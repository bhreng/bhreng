## 1. The shop safety test — full contents

`BHR Engineering Shop Safety Test`, Google Form, **15 points, two sections**.
It opens: *"Please complete the following after reading through the BHR
Engineering Makerspace Rules."* Emails are collected automatically.

### Section 1 — five short answers

1. What is the extension for the school nurse? *(4-digit number)*
2. Access to the Makerspace must be granted by an…
3. How many students are allowed in the Makerspace?
4. Only ____________ students are permitted to operate machinery and/or hand
   tools. *(select all that appear on the rules)*
5. What should you do if you do not know something?

### Section 2 — ten "which rule theme" items

Students pick 1–5 for each of these:

- Think through the entire job before starting…
- Keep fingers clear of the point of operation of machines…
- Wear only shoes that cover the entire foot…
- Never use a tool unless you've been trained to use it safely.
- Do not fool around, startle, or distract anyone…
- Do not attempt to remove foreign objects from the eye or body.
- Never use a rag near moving machinery.
- Safety is your top priority… If you are not sure what you are doing, ask.
- Wear eye protection… at all times, whether working or not.
- Know all the locations of all first aid, fire, and safety equipment.

### RESOLVED, 4 September 2026

**There are two rules documents, and I only had one.** Dan supplied
*Primary Makerspace Rules* — ten numbered conditions of entry, plus a maximum
occupancy of twelve and the nurse extension. It answers all three questions:

- **Access** is granted by an instructor (rule 1), and may be revoked at any time
  at an instructor's discretion (rule 2).
- **Occupancy** is twelve — but Dan's own gloss is that it is a judgement rather
  than a turnstile: if the room goes over, whether it carries on is the
  instructor's call.
- **Who may operate machinery** — only *trained and authorized* students (rule 8).
  The document defines both: trained means the provided training is complete;
  authorized means the appropriate safety test has been passed.

That last one is the keystone. The trained/authorized split is the same idea as
the Bambu certification ladder, generalised to every machine in the shop, and it
is now the organising principle of the safety section.

Both documents are on the site: the ten primary rules first as the conditions of
entry, then the thirty-two working rules as "the complete set".

### The original problem, for the record

**Three of the five Section 1 questions could not be answered from the *working*
rules document.** I transcribed that PDF in full — all 32 rules — and it contains
nothing about:

- who grants access to the Makerspace,
- how many students are allowed in at once,
- which students may operate machinery and hand tools.

The test explicitly tells students to answer it *after reading through the
Makerspace Rules*. So either there is a second, fuller rules document I have not
found, or the test has drifted away from the document it points at. Two questions
*are* answerable: the nurse extension (2230) and "if you do not know, ask."

**This needs your call before the safety section goes live.** Either the rules
document is missing an access-control section, or the test is asking for things
it never taught.

### What was built from it

`shop/themes.html` — the Section 2 exercise, live on the site. All ten rules
verbatim from your Form, with the correct theme derived from where each rule
actually sits in the rules document, so nothing is guessed. It uses the theme
**names** rather than the numbers 1–5, because the numbering is an artefact of
the paper layout and the names are what a student should carry into the shop.

Note that of the ten items, **none** fall under theme 4, "Use tools right" —
worth knowing if you ever want the practice to cover all five evenly.

Also found: roughly **fifteen copies** of the test and acknowledgement form
across the Classroom folders, one per class per year. And three copies of
`1.1.1.A GeneralSafetyTest.docx`, which is PLTW — untouched, flagged only so it
is not mistaken for a source.
