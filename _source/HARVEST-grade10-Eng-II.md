## EOY Project — the pullback car redesign

**Design work.** Students work individually to redesign a simple pullback car
to achieve a **measurable improvement in a chosen performance criterion**. The
project emphasises efficient design, documentation and focused application of
CAD skills.

**Analysis and problem definition** — disassemble the pullback car and create
an inventory of parts. Create 2D profile drawings of key parts in AutoCAD,
focusing on parts likely to be modified. Document the car's original
performance (baseline testing) using a simplified procedure. Identify one or
two key limitations of the original design.

**Concept generation and selection** — brainstorm and sketch at least two
distinct concepts. Use a simplified decision matrix or weighted criteria table
to evaluate them. Select one and begin detailed sketches.

*Deliverables, phases 1–2:* AutoCAD drawings of key parts; baseline testing
data; an engineering notebook with initial sketches and concept selection.

**Design development and CAD modelling** — detailed sketches of the final
design; a Fusion 360 or Inventor model with renderings; a simplified
orthographic drawing sheet.

**Prototype construction and testing** — build a prototype focused on modifying
one or two key components. Conduct performance tests and compare with baseline
data. Document process and results in the engineering notebook.

**Presentation and documentation** — a brief presentation, plus the engineering
notebook, design brief / initial planner, part list, and CAD files and
drawings.

*Deliverables, phases 3–5:* the prototype, testing data and analysis, the final
presentation, and all project documentation.

**EOY Project — Documents.** The end-of-year capstone encompasses everything
done to this point as an engineering student, going through every stage of the
engineering design process and producing every form of shop documentation
introduced so far, plus a few new ones. Documents to complete: **design brief /
initial planner, part list, decision matrix, Gantt chart.**

## CrunchLabs Kit Re-Engineering Challenge

Creatively repurpose components from disassembled CrunchLabs kits to design and
build a new functional device, or significantly improve an existing one. The
project emphasises innovation, problem-solving, resourcefulness, and the
integration of digital modelling with physical construction.

**Inventory and ideation** — take inventory of available components: engraved
pieces, 3D printed parts, electronic components such as motors, fasteners and
other hardware. Brainstorm: what problems can you solve, what new functions can
you create, how can you combine components in unique ways?

**Design and modelling in Fusion 360** — a detailed plan that MUST include
sketches of the proposed device to guide the modelling; identification of all
components, existing and new, in a part list; 3D models of all parts, **each
part modelled in its own separate Fusion 360 file**; all parts assembled into a
single assembly file demonstrating how they fit together and function; and an
explanation of how the device will function, including how new parts integrate
with existing CrunchLabs components.

**Construction and modification** — build according to the design, modify
existing components as needed, 3D print the parts you designed, engrave any
necessary 2D parts. Pay attention to build quality and stability.

**Testing and refinement** — test thoroughly, identify weaknesses, refine.

**Presentation** — demonstrate the device's function, explain the design
process, and display your work.

## Architectural Research Design Project

A week on architecture: explore iconic structures and develop design and
digital modelling skills. All work submitted digitally; physical drawings
uploaded to Classroom **and** placed on the instructor's desk.

**Monday — research and presentation.** Watch the 45-minute video on architect
Bjarke Ingels for inspiration and context, then research your chosen structure:
name and location; architects and date of construction; purpose and function;
key architectural features and design elements; historical and cultural
context; and your reasons for choosing it. Compile into Google Slides —
informative and engaging, because you present it after lunch.

**Tuesday — sketching and drawing.** Entirely sketching. **Absolutely no CAD
work today.** Multiple views (plan, elevation, section), different angles and
perspectives, detailed drawings of specific architectural elements, floor plans
if relevant. Annotate with labels, dimensions and notes explaining design
choices and observations. Level of detail is up to you, but a full day's worth
of dedicated drawing is expected. Independent study on drawing techniques is
welcome.

**Wednesday to Friday — 3D modelling.** Recreate the structure in a program of
your choice (Revit, SketchUp, Fusion 360). Pace yourself: start with a basic
model and build up gradually.

**Objective** — accurately recreate the provided 10×12 lean-to office shed

## Lego SumoBot Design

Design, build and program a Lego SumoBot to battle other robots in the ring,
covering mechanical design, programming and SumoBot strategy.

**Step 1 — design and build.** Review the SumoBot strategy video and the
programming tutorial. Sketch initial designs considering mobility, power,
defense, offense, and the size and weight limits in the posted rules. Select
your best design and build, iterating as you go.

**Step 2 — program and test.** Integrate sensors (touch, distance) and write
code to control movement and reactions. Test against others or in the practice
ring, refining code and mechanics.

**Step 3 — documentation, CAD and competition.** A concise Google Doc with
design rationale, coding, and Lego parts used. CAD: either a set of 2D drawings
(colour, labelled dimensions) or a Fusion 360 model. Then the battles.

**Constraints** — adhere to size and weight limits; Lego parts only.

**SumoBot CAD Work.** A full set of 2D CAD drawings in colour, or a Fusion 360
model. Each unique view (top, front, side, rear) must be represented — a 3D
model does this automatically. Level of detail: each piece, in colour, must be
distinguishable from another. Unless connection pieces are visible, ignore
them. Suggestion: work in AutoCAD unless you are confident of finishing a 3D
model in under a week. Level of detail is **piece, shape and colour** — no need
to go beyond unless time allows.

**Experimental Design Sheet.** Complete the sheet for your SumoBot trials.
Trial 1: can it stay in the circle? Trial 2: can it push an object out of the
circle? Trial 3: how heavy an object can it push?

## RE-Pitch an Invention

Embody the spirit of an inventor and deliver a persuasive "Shark Tank" style
pitch for an existing invention or idea.

**Selection** — choose an existing invention or idea you find interesting and
believe has strong potential for a successful pitch.

**Inventor's deep dive and market analysis** — thoroughly research the
invention, its inventor, its history, its intended purpose and original
context. Analyse the current market: target audience, potential competitors,
market trends. Develop realistic **financial projections** including potential
revenue, expenses and profit margins, considering scalability and growth.

**Presentation** — dynamic and persuasive, mirroring a Shark Tank pitch, with a
captivating introduction of the invention and its inventor, a clear explanation
of its purpose and functionality, and a compelling demonstration of the need it
addresses.

**Annotated sketch** — recreate or create a detailed sketch reflecting the
original invention, annotated with the key features and the inventor's thought
process.

**3D model** — an accurate Fusion 360 representation capturing the essence of
the original design and functionality.

## Furniture in a Box

Design or recreate a piece of furniture in Fusion 360. Shelving or storage
preferred, and it needs approval before proceeding. **Focus on part creation,
not on modelling the whole assembly.**

- Start with brainstorming and conceptual design work
- Create **each part** of the furniture, all the way down to the hardware
- Design the box and packaging it comes in
- An exploded assembly animation **coming out of the box**
- An exploded assembly animation **from assembled to unassembled**
- Work in multiple files: a project folder with each part as a separate file

**Final products** — renderings, multi-views, two animations, and more.

## How to Build a House

A collaborative exploration of residential construction, watching a YouTube
series that follows the construction of a simple two-storey house. Spans a
class and possibly into Wednesday.

As you progress through the videos, each student develops their own set of
slides detailing the key steps in the building process. You work as a group,
taking turns leading the class discussion and clarifying information between
videos — but **the completed slides must be your own work** and are submitted
individually.

The outcome is a comprehensive slide show outlining the entire process from
foundation to finish.

> "You can watch the 'Bonus' episodes if you want, but do not do slides for
