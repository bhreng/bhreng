## Extracted structures

### Engineering Do Now! — Reflection

The standard companion to every Do Now. Confirms the reflection is part of the
format rather than an occasional extra.

- **Title for Do Now!**
- **Equipment used** — bulleted list
- **New Knowledge Acquired** — skills/concepts, bulleted
- **Brief Process Summary** — a few sentences on the steps followed to use each
  new skill
- **Future Project Application** — how this applies to the current or upcoming
  main project
- **Confidence Level** — self-assessment of ability to use the skill again

### Mid-Project Design Review & Feasibility Check

Inserted partway through every substantial project.

- **Phase of Project** — Planning / Design / Development
- **Remaining Tasks to Complete**
- **Conceptual Design Justification** — why this concept over the alternatives;
  which decisions are critical or risky; why they will hold
- **Feasibility & Risk Check** — viability against time, budget and specialised
  knowledge; the resource posing the greatest challenge; the single element
  carrying the highest risk of failure that must be tested to prove the concept
- **Feedback & Action Items** — which feedback paths were used (one-on-one,
  peer review, other) and the next steps to validate or redesign

### Grade 11 Capstone — the full structure

Two projects run simultaneously over 15 days.

**Project 1 — A Unique Concept.** An original structure that stretches
traditional design (upside-down buildings, integrated vertical ecosystems) which
the student must *prove* is viable.
Deliverables: Engineering Logic Report (research on comparable structures or
principles), Structural Analysis (Fusion 360 or other simulation to model
forces, moments, stability), Feasibility Testing (physical or digital data
validating the unique structural aspect).

**Project 2 — The Revitalization.** An existing area or structure transformed —
"not just renovating; transforming how people connect with that space."
Deliverables: the Professional Pitch (before/after transformations with
reasoning), Design Documents (site plans, floor plans, elevations, sections),
Visual Storytelling (3D renders or physical scale models).

**Week 1 — Conceptual Design.** Monday planner defines both directions.
Research doc with at least three credible sources validating the physics.
Instructor consultation on structural viability. Two real revitalization
precedents researched. Deliverables: annotated sketches, initial CAD massing,
optional chipboard model. Friday reflection.

**Week 2 — Detailed Design.** Heavy CAD. Load requirements identified; stress
and deflection analysed. Site plan plus two further views, annotated and
dimensioned. Deliverables: PDF design document set, multiple high-quality
renders, optionally a preliminary BOM and estimate — "the Business of
Engineering." Friday reflection.

**Week 3 — Validation, Optimisation & Defence.** Fabrication scheduled to avoid
bottlenecks. Proof-of-concept finalised as the core justification. Pitch
polished. Deliverables: all design work, final prototypes and simulation data,
comprehensive final presentation covering the 15-day journey. Final reflection.

**Roughly fourteen YouTube links** are embedded across the three weeks, three to
four per week, as the supporting instruction. These are strong candidates for
the Skill Library — they are exactly the "skill-building videos" pattern.
