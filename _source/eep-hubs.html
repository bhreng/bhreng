What your Independent Focus is

The Independent Focus replaces what was called Independent Study: self-directed, term-length work in an emerging engineering field. Every student in the shop has one; it ends when the Senior Capstone starts. It classifies as Applied Learning and is the primary vehicle for Standard 12 — Advanced Elective Area.

The hubs are not courses. Grade-level classrooms remain the instructional and assessment centers; the hubs are specialized resource libraries students draw on for research and deep specialization.

The shared five-topic structure

Every hub uses the same five topics. Each answers one question, and the question is the sorting test — if a resource doesn't answer it, it belongs in a different topic.

TopicSorting questionWhat belongs here
Explore the FieldWould this help someone decide whether to pick this pathway?Careers, roles, what practitioners actually do, where the discipline leads
Learn the ConceptsIs this something you study to understand how the work works?Textbooks, theory, principles, the science behind the decisions
Build Your SkillsIs this a procedure you practice until you can do it?Software walkthroughs, equipment training, technique
Find a ProjectIs this something you could go build?Design briefs, challenges, project seeds
Get the FilesWould you open this while working, rather than read it start to finish?Templates, forms, rubrics, safety rules

Why the names changed

These were Pathway Foundational Knowledge, Pathway Learning, Skill Training, Idea Lab and Resource Hub. The first two read as synonyms, which is why sorting drifted: when a boundary is unclear, everything lands in the first bucket. Of the 70 sources sorted for Industrial Design, 48 went to Foundational and none to Skill Training — in the pathway where Fusion 360 competency is the whole point.

The underlying five buckets were right. The names and the sorting test are what needed fixing.

Findings across all seven

The Automation / Robotics guide is empty

Not thin — blank. The file exists and returns no content at all. This is the pathway the program's own crosswalk describes as the primary synthesis point for mechatronics and CIM, drawing on Standards 5, 6, 7 and 9 together. The most interdisciplinary pathway has no guide.

Correction to an earlier finding

I previously flagged Standard 11 as an uncovered gap because no core course produces a Business Plan. That was incomplete. The Mechanical, Electrical and Project Management hub guides all require one — Project Management specifies it in detail, applied to an emerging engineering field with design, market potential and implementation strategy. The business plan lives in the Independent Focus, not the core courses. Standard 14 (Entrepreneurship) is arguably covered by the same work.

Every guide uses retired terminology

All six written guides say "Engineering Notebook" and "Engineering Daily Journal." Both were replaced by the Engineering Logbook this year. Given the guides also carry the strictest documentation rules in the program, the naming needs to match what students actually open.

Three different logbook rule sets

Industrial Design cites a "Rule of 11" from University of Georgia standards. Architecture/Civil cites "17 strict rules" from the same source. Mechanical cites Kelley & Wicklein. Project Management cites Kelley plus the Tesla-versus-Marconi case. Same underlying practice, four presentations, different counts. A student in two pathways gets two rulebooks.

Two things to verify before students see them

Salary figures. The Project Management guide states $120,000+ for Management Engineer, $98,000+ for Construction Manager, $92,000+ for Systems Integrator, and projects 6–10% growth. No source, no date, no region. Formatting corruption. Section 6 of the same guide has raw escape characters visible in the text — \#\#\#\#\# and \*\* appearing as literal characters.

The five topics

01

Explore the Field

Careers: Product Design, Manufacturing Engineering, Technical Drafting, Research Engineer, Development Engineer — what each role is responsible for and where it leads after graduation.

02

Learn the Concepts

User-centered design and human factors. DFM theory split into additive geometry (3D printing) and subtractive geometry (laser, CNC). Visual communication of design intent. Engineering Accounting from Holtzapple & Reece — scientific principles as a unifying framework for design decisions.

Texts: Wallach's Modern Drafting for descriptive geometry and ASME standards; Madsen's Engineering Drawing and Design for visualization. (Moved here from topic 1 — textbooks are concepts you study, not orientation to the field.)

03

Build Your Skills

Fusion 360 — parametric (history-driven), direct modeling (rapid iteration), and visualization (renders, animations, exploded views). Equipment vault: 3D scanners for reverse engineering, 3D printers, laser engravers and CNC.

04

Find a Project

Named briefs: Desk Organizer and Kids Toy. Four-phase loop — Identify & Research, Develop & Plan (massing), Create (digital then physical), Test & Redesign. Deliverable is a "before and after" transformation case study, explicitly an exercise in visual storytelling.

05

Get the Files

Documentation as a legal single source of truth. Templates: logbook, BOM worksheets, reflections, makerspace safety rules.

The Rule of 11

The strictest documentation practice in any pathway, from University of Georgia standards:

  • Permanent ink only. No pencils, no erasures. Errors get a single X.
  • Chronological integrity. No pages skipped; every page numbered, signed, dated.
  • Taping loose sheets. External data taped to the next available page — tape every corner and sign across the tape, so the signature begins on the loose sheet and ends on the page.
  • Witness signatures. Entries witnessed by a peer with the technical ability to understand the work.

Operational rhythm

A mandatory two-hour weekly block for Knowledge Consumption and Skill Training. Regular design reviews with Mr. Frank covering manufacturability, GD&T accuracy and aesthetic finish. Culminates in a professional defense with physical evidence of fabrication quality.

Only pathway with a source library

Industrial Design has a Source Migration Table of 70 resources already sorted into topics — MIT computational geometry, NASA human-factors standards, MIL-STD-1472, Fusion 360 tutorials, TED talks. No other pathway has one. Two caveats: the hyperlink column may not contain live URLs, and the sort is lopsided — 48 of 70 landed in Foundational Knowledge and none in Skill Training, despite this being the pathway where Fusion 360 competency gets built.

The professional site design checklist

Every site plan must include:

  • Soil testing — documented analysis of results
  • Cut and fill calculations — accurate volumes for grading
  • Drainage — engineered plans for water runoff
  • Circulation — parking, roads and ADA accessibility

This is the most concrete, checkable deliverable list in any of the seven guides.

Operational detail to verify

This guide names the school nurse's extension (Ex. 2230) in its emergency procedure. Worth confirming it's still current — it's the kind of detail that silently goes stale and matters most on the day it's needed.

Pathway · Standard 7 · PCC 7, 8

Mechanical Engineering

Kinematics, statics and parametric mastery — from manual vector resolution to Finite Element Analysis.

Lead
Mr. Frank (EDF)
Standard
7, 11, 12
Credential
ACU Inventor. The source guide also named SolidWorks CSWA; struck — the shop is Autodesk only.
Prerequisite
OSHA 10

The five topics

01

Explore the Field

Engineering Accounting stated as an actual equation — Input − Output + Generation − Consumption = Accumulation — tracking mass, energy and momentum across any mechanical system. Newton's three laws framed by function: inertia as the foundation of statics, F=ma for dynamic systems, action-reaction for resolving joints and fasteners.

02

Learn the Concepts

Vector resolution, kinematic equations, free body diagrams, stress and strain. The key pedagogical link: manual vector calculations are the mathematical precursor to the mesh nodes a digital solver uses. Materials classification and Mean Time Between Failure for reliability.

03

Build Your Skills

Tiered: Inventor (ACU) for parametric modeling, then Fusion 360 (ACP) for assemblies, simulation and GD&T. Measurement and calibration — calipers, micrometers, multimeters, oscilloscopes, all calibrated against known standards before data collection.

04

Find a Project

Three named challenges. Parametric optimization — one driving dimension updates all related components. Functional prototype loop — FEA-simulate a bracket's failure point, print it, test it, analyse the variance between simulation and reality. Simple machine integration — three machines minimum, mechanical advantage calculated and verified.

05

Get the Files

The logbook as legal document per Kelley & Wicklein — hardbound, numbered, ink only, signed and witnessed. Safety non-negotiables including the 15-minute eye-wash rule and the two-person rule for power tools.

Best-articulated idea in any guide

The insistence that design decisions be data-driven: "Intuition is a starting point, but Improve & Redesign phases require objective evidence from FEA reports or physical test logs to justify modifications." That sentence does more work than most of the vision statements in the other guides.

Mastery requirements

Standard 11 — a lifecycle analysis and detailed business plan for a design in an emerging field, covering market potential, implementation strategy and interdisciplinary coordination.

Standard 12 — a mandatory reverse engineering task: select a mechanical system, analyse component interactions, recreate it digitally, and validate with stress and motion simulation.

Where to go for what

Mr. Dryer is named for four specific things — worth reproducing because it's the clearest "ask me about" list in any guide:

  • Logic analysis — developing, simplifying and verifying Boolean expressions and truth tables
  • Debugging — syntax errors or logical flaws in Python, C++ (Arduino) or ladder logic
  • System architecture — designing the data flow of automated systems
  • Functional simulation — predicting mechanical behavior through digital models

Two documents for one pathway

Software Engineering has both a Hub Guide (May 2026) and a separate Resource Directory and Guide (June 2026) — overlapping genres. The June document matches the newer pattern used for Industrial Design's source directory. Decide whether guide and directory are one document or two, then apply that answer to all seven pathways.

Electrical Engineering

Analog circuits, Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws, and test-device proficiency — from electron flow to intelligent system performance.

Lead
Mr. Dryer (ESEC)
Standard
5, 11, 12
Credential
Multimeter / oscilloscope
Prerequisite

The five topics

01

Explore the Field

Careers: Electrical Engineering, Robotics Engineering, Systems Integration. Atomic structure and its role in electrical properties. Materials as conductors, insulators, semiconductors. The four circuit components — source, load, control, conductors. Ohm's Law as the cornerstone relationship.

02

Learn the Concepts

DC versus AC and where each is used. Series, parallel and series-parallel configurations. Kirchhoff's current and voltage laws. Resistor colour codes for identification without instruments. Renewable energy applications — conversion efficiency for solar and wind. Text: Tokheim's Digital Electronics.

03

Build Your Skills

Multimeter proficiency across V, I, R and C. Oscilloscope mastery — square, sawtooth and sine waveforms, determining time, fall time, frequency and amplitude. Platforms: Arduino Education, Festo LX.

04

Find a Project

Sensor and actuator integration — systems that sense environmental variables and execute mechanical responses. Troubleshooting challenges across integrated hardware and software. A data-driven redesign loop.

05

Get the Files

Documentation as legal record of intellectual property. Electrical safety: GFCIs, immediate reporting of frayed wiring, and strict Lockout/Tagout compliance for de-energizing equipment.

The breadboarding walkthrough

The most usable procedure in any of the seven guides — five steps a student can follow unsupervised:

  • Interpret the schematic — translate the diagram into a physical component layout
  • Breadboard setup — insert components, strictly observing polarity for diodes and electrolytic capacitors
  • Calibration — self-test and calibrate the multimeter and oscilloscope before measuring
  • Connectivity — link source, control and load with appropriate gauge conductors
  • Initial power-on testing — verify continuity and node voltage before full energization

Mastery requirements

Standard 11 — a comprehensive business plan: market potential, implementation strategy, lifecycle analysis.

Standard 12 — a reverse engineering report on a complex component, including a repair log and maintenance strategy.

Everything below is reconstructed from the Standards-to-Hub Crosswalk and the Program Implementation Report, not from a guide.

What the program says this pathway is

The crosswalk describes Automation/Robotics as the program's primary synthesis point — the place where the two specializations genuinely have to work together rather than dividing the work.

Technical focus

  • Mechatronics and Computer Integrated Manufacturing (CIM)
  • PLC programming and robotic controller integration
  • End-of-arm tooling, sensor and actuator feedback loops

Why it draws on four standards at once

StandardContribution
5 · ElectricalAnalog circuit requirements, power distribution, sensor wiring
6 · DigitalLogical frameworks, Boolean design, PLC programming for system intelligence
7 · MechanicalDrive systems, kinematics of work envelopes, physical chassis fabrication
9 · Automated SystemsThe anchor — CIM components, robotic controllers, the CNC-to-robot relationship

This is also the only pathway with shared instructional leadership: Mr. Frank guides physical fabrication, structural integrity and manufacturability; Mr. Dryer covers code efficiency, circuit troubleshooting and analytical validation of control loops.

Where the content would come from

Grade 12 Unit 4 (Robotics and Automation) is the richest existing source — Section 7 maps 19 skills to it: S9-c, m, o, q, t, u, v, w, x, y, z, aa, ae, af, ag, ah, ai, aj, ak. That covers end effectors, CIM operation, drive system integration, cybersecurity for automated systems, Industry 4.0 and digital twins, and collaborative robot programming.

Grade 11 Unit 5 (Control Systems & Mechatronics) adds twelve more: S9-b, d, e, f, g, h, i, l, p, r, s, ab.

Between them, the curriculum content for this pathway already exists and is standards-mapped. It has simply never been written into a guide.

Open question

Is this blank because it's Mr. Dryer's to write, or did it get dropped in the May batch? It's the only one of the seven with shared leadership, which may be exactly why nobody owned it.

The five topics

Explore the Field

Develops the Management Engineer — synthesizing technical design with business planning so projects are economically viable as well as functional. Roles: Management Engineer (shared), Construction Manager (Frank), Systems Integrator (Dryer).

02

Learn the Concepts

The EDP as a non-linear cycle. Work Breakdown Structure — hierarchical decomposition of scope into deliverables. Decision matrices for weighing competing solutions against weighted constraints. Requirements analysis separating user needs from functional specifications.

03

Build Your Skills

WBS then Gantt: decompose the capstone into phases, map onto a timeline, identify the critical path — the task sequence that pushes the final defense date if it slips. Track lead time for material procurement in the BOM. Brilliant.org for the mathematical rigor behind critical path method.

04

Find a Project

The Junior Capstone Revitalization project as the primary case study — students act as lead developers pitching to town stakeholders. Checklist: before-and-after transformations, technical justification, visual storytelling with 3D renders or scale models, and reverse-engineering documentation.

05

Get the Files

Weekly planner with milestones and resource allocation. BOM for procurement — part number, description, material, quantity, unit cost. Logbook and term-end reflections.

Standard 11 — the business plan

Specified here in more detail than anywhere else: a business plan applied to an emerging engineering field — aerospace, sustainable tech, advanced manufacturing — requiring a CAD-validated technical model, data-driven market research, and a procurement and manufacturing roadmap with scaling logistics.

Metacognitive coding

The source guide had students tag logbook entries with a mix of EDP codes (PI) and pillar codes (AL, Applied Learning). Reconciled: the Daily Logbook now uses one scheme only — the five EDP status codes PI, DD, FAB, TE, IR — and the pillar codes stay in the binder where they belong.
