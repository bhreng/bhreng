**What this class is.** An idea bank, filed under the shop's own core-concept
pillars rather than by term. Almost every item is a *material* — a title with an
attachment (usually a YouTube video) and no description text. I opened a sample
and confirmed: the bodies are empty, so the titles below are the content. That
also means this is the one class where the value is in the attachments, and
those are one click away in Classroom rather than extractable in bulk.

**Why the structure matters.** The topics are the EDF/ESEC pillars from the
curriculum documents. So this class is already a map of "which pillar is
under-resourced" — and two pillars are visibly empty.

## What the shape of this tells you

**Three pillars are empty:** Software & Algorithmic Engineering, Empirical
Analysis & Optimization, and Professionalism & Safe Operations. Two of those
are Mr. Dryer's half of the axis, and the third is the one the whole shop
depends on.

**The manufacturing pillar is the deepest by far** — ten items, all of them
practical fabrication technique: DFMA, snap fits, heat-set inserts,
print-in-place, injection moulding comparison. That is a genuinely strong
resource shelf and the most reusable thing in this class.

**Several items here are already live assignments elsewhere** — RE-Pitch an
Invention is a Grade 10 project, VEX AIM Bot and Reverse Engineering Breadboard
are Grade 12. So the class doubles as a staging area: ideas move from here into
a grade's Classroom.

**One item is an explicit curriculum proposal:** "City Miniature Project —
possible freshmen EOY replacement". Worth a decision rather than leaving it in
an idea bank, since the Grade 9 class currently runs an EOY urban planning
project that sounds like the thing it would replace.
