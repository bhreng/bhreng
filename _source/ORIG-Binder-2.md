Our Engineering Technology program is structured around five interconnected pillars, designed to provide a holistic and industry-relevant education. These pillars guide all student activities, from daily "Do Nows" to capstone projects, ensuring a comprehensive learning experience that integrates theoretical knowledge, practical application, professional skills, and continuous evaluation.

## 1. Knowledge Consumption (KC)

- **Definition:** This pillar focuses on the **acquisition of new information, concepts, and foundational procedural skills.** It's about understanding "the why" and "the how-to" before application. Students actively engage with new material through various formats, building their theoretical base and skill repertoire.
- **Key Activities:**
    - **Do Now!:** Micro-learning tasks for quick skill/concept introduction or review.
    - **Theory-Based Lessons:** In-depth study of scientific principles, engineering concepts, analytical methods, or historical context.
    - **Training-Based Lessons:** Instructor-led or self-guided practice to acquire specific procedural skills, software proficiency, or initial equipment operation.
- **Instructor Specialization:**
    - **EDF (Engineering Design & Fabrication):** Focuses on principles of Industrial Design, Drafting Standards (GD&T), DFM theory for additive/subtractive geometry, architectural and structural concepts, advanced CAD modeling techniques, and 3D printing/laser engraving theory.
    - **ESEC (Engineering Systems & Emerging Concepts):** Focuses on Digital Electronics, Logic Design, Algorithmic Analysis, Coding (Arduino, Python, PLC), Systems Engineering principles, Mechatronics, Electrical Engineering Fundamentals, Quantum Computing Concepts, Sensor Theory, Materials Science, and Finite Element Analysis (FEA) theory.
- **Relevant Engineering Roles Emphasized:** Primarily **Research Engineer** (investigating principles), and aspects of **Design Engineer** / **Development Engineer** (learning new tools/methods).
- **EDP Stages Supported:** Primarily *Identify the Problem* (understanding context) and *Research & Brainstorm* (gathering information).

## 2. Applied Learning (AL)

- **Definition:** This pillar is where students actively **implement acquired knowledge and skills to solve problems, create designs, develop systems, and produce tangible outcomes.** It's the "doing" phase, characterized by hands-on engagement, experimentation, and iterative development.
- **Key Activities:**
    - **Project Work:** Extended activities applying a range of skills to design, develop, test, and produce a tangible outcome.
    - **Assignment:** Focused tasks applying knowledge to specific challenges (e.g., design briefs, problem sets, small fabrication tasks).
    - **Lab & Testing:** Hands-on activities for experimenting, data collection, functional tests, and troubleshooting.
    - **Independent Study:** Self-directed, long-term projects demonstrating comprehensive application of skills in research, design, or development.

- **Relevant Engineering Roles Emphasized:** **Design Engineer** (conceptualization to detailed design), **Development Engineer** (functional prototyping, system integration), **Production & Construction Engineer** (fabrication, assembly).
- **EDP Stages Supported:** Primarily *Develop Solutions*, *Plan a Solution*, *Create (Model or Prototype)*, *Test and Evaluate*, and *Improve and Redesign*.

## 3. Safety & Operations (S&O)

- **Definition:** This pillar instills the critical importance of **ensuring a secure, functional, and well-maintained learning and working environment.** It covers protocols for equipment use, shop organization, and responsible stewardship.
- **Key Activities:**
    - **Training-Based Lesson:** Specific sessions on machine safety, PPE usage, shop protocols, and emergency procedures.
    - **Shop Stewardship:** Activities dedicated to maintaining the shop, tool organization, and equipment calibration/cleaning.
    - **Integrated within Project Work/Lab & Testing:** Continuous adherence to safety and operational guidelines during all hands-on tasks.
- **Instructor Specialization:** While a shared responsibility, specific S&O tasks often align:
    - **EDF:** Focus on 3D printer operation, laser engraver safety, safe use of manual fabrication tools, and digital file management.
    - **ESEC:** Focus on power tools, electrical safety, machine tool operation (e.g., CNC, mill), and tool maintenance procedures.
- **Relevant Engineering Roles Emphasized:** Primarily **Production & Construction Engineer** (safe and efficient execution), and **Operations Engineer** (system reliability, maintenance).
- **EDP Stages Supported:** Crucial during *Plan a Solution* (safety considerations) and *Create (Model or Prototype)* (safe execution).

## 4. Professional Practice (PP)

- **Definition:** This pillar develops the **non-technical, "soft skills" and professional attributes** essential for success in any engineering career. It includes teamwork, communication, ethics, and foundational project management.
- **Key Activities:**
    - **Professional Development Activities (PDA):** Workshops on teamwork, communication strategies, ethics discussions, or project management methodologies.
    - **One-on-One Meeting / Mentoring:** Individualized discussions for personalized guidance, project advice, or career counseling.
    - **Integrated within Project Work:** Collaboration within teams, technical communication, and adherence to professional standards.
- **Instructor Specialization:** Often delivered jointly or integrated into AL with specific emphases:
    - **EDF:** Focus on design critiques, visual communication (presentations), user-centered design, and design management.
    - **ESEC:** Focus on technical documentation, systematic troubleshooting protocols, requirements analysis, and technical reporting.
- **Relevant Engineering Roles Emphasized:** **Management Engineer** (planning, leading), **Sales Engineer** (communication, value proposition), **Operations Engineer** (process optimization, collaboration), and all roles (ethics, teamwork).
- **EDP Stages Supported:** Integral to *Identify the Problem* (user needs), *Research & Brainstorm* (collaboration), *Plan a Solution* (team planning), and *Present Your Solution*.

## 5. Assessment & Reflection (A&R)

- **Definition:** This pillar focuses on **evaluating learning outcomes, documenting progress, and fostering metacognition** – the ability to think about one's own learning and problem-solving processes. It ensures accountability and promotes continuous improvement.
- **Key Activities:**
    - **Project Presentation/Review:** Formal or informal showcases of work, explaining processes, and receiving feedback.
    - **Quiz / Test:** Formal assessments of comprehension and recall.
    - **Project Reflections:** Mid-point and end-of-project self-assessments (e.g., Independent Study reflections).
    - **"Do Now!" Reflection:** Quick self-assessments on newly acquired knowledge/skills.
    - **Engineering Notebooks:** Documenting daily progress, challenges, and solutions.   ← *retired term; now the Engineering Logbook*
- **Instructor Specialization:** The *assigned instructor* leads grading and feedback for their groups, drawing on expertise from both specializations to evaluate:
    - **EDF:** Design quality, drafting accuracy, evidence of iteration, aesthetic appeal, and fabrication quality.
    - **ESEC:** Functional success, analytical rigor, code efficiency, system performance, and adherence to specifications.
- **Relevant Engineering Roles Emphasized:** All roles contribute to documentation and evaluation. Particularly **Research Engineer** (analyzing results), **Design Engineer** / **Development Engineer** (iterative feedback loops), and **Management Engineer** (assessing progress).
- **EDP Stages Supported:** Crucial during *Test and Evaluate* (analyzing results) and *Present Your Solution* (communicating outcomes).
