# 1.0 Purpose

This handbook is the instructor's guide to the BHR Engineering Technology program. It sets out the structure, curriculum, and assessment approach so that instruction is consistent across both specializations and all four grade levels.

The program prepares students for competitive technical colleges and for engineering careers by taking them through the complete engineering lifecycle — conceptual design, fabrication, systems integration, and analysis.

# 2.0 The Framework in Practice

Sections 1–4 define the program's four moving parts: two instructor specializations, five pillars, ten content categories, and a set of classified task types. This section is where they meet.

The short version: **the pillars are the *how*, the PCCs are the *what*, and the specializations determine *who* leads.** Every activity a student does can be located on all three axes.

## 2.1 Activity Types by Pillar

| Activity Type | Primary Pillar | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Do Now! | KC (secondary A&R) | Short focused activity — a micro-skill or key concept. |
| Theory-Based Lesson | KC | Fundamental principles and concepts. The "why." |
| Training-Based Lesson | KC **or** S&O | Procedural skill, software proficiency, or safety protocol. S&O when the subject is equipment safety or setup. |
| Project Work | AL (secondary S&O, PP, A&R) | Extended multi-day application producing a tangible outcome. |
| Assignment | AL, sometimes KC or PP | Depends on the task — a research summary is KC, a technical report is PP. |
| Lab & Testing | AL (secondary S&O, A&R) | Experimentation, data collection, functional testing, troubleshooting. |
| **Independent Focus** | AL (secondary KC, PP, A&R) | Self-directed, term-length work in an emerging field. Upper years. See 2.3. |
| Shop Stewardship | S&O | Tool organization, equipment maintenance, workspace restoration. |
| Professional Development Activity | PP | Teamwork, communication, ethics, project management. |
| One-on-One Meeting / Mentoring | PP **or** A&R | PP for career and project advice; A&R for feedback and progress. |
| Project Presentation / Review | A&R (secondary PP) | Formal or informal showcase with feedback. |
| Project Reflection | A&R | Mid-point and end-of-project self-assessment. |
| Engineering Logbook | A&R (secondary PP) | Daily documentation. See 4.3. |
| Quiz / Test | A&R | Formal assessment of comprehension or recall. |

> Full definitions of each task type are in **Section 4**.

## 2.3 The Independent Focus

The Independent Focus is the program's self-directed track for every student in the shop, built around **the seven Engineering Pathways**. It replaces what was previously called Independent Study.

Within the framework it classifies as **Applied Learning** — a self-directed, term-length project in a chosen specialization, drawing on Knowledge Consumption from both instructors while the assigned instructor oversees the applied work and assessment. It is the primary vehicle for **Standard 12 (Advanced Elective Area)**.

Seven pathways are available: Industrial Design, Architecture/Civil Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Software Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Automation/Robotics, and Project Management.

# 4.0 Assessment, Grading, and Documentation

## 4.1 Grading Policy

| Category | Description | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Grade** | Individual rubrics per project. Graded on productivity, creativity, accuracy, and timing. | 35% |
| **Weekly Grade** | Behavior and performance: safety, initiative, preparation, attitude, work ethic, productivity. | 30% |
| **Classwork Assignments** | Classroom exercises — pacing, staying on task, following directions. | 15% |
| **Employability** | Weekly and term-based soft skills: recurring assignments and shop professionalism. | 20% |

> The Weekly Grade and Employability categories together carry **50%** of the course grade. Both map to **Standard 13 (Employability Skills)**.

## 4.2 Weekly Grade Rubric

Six criteria. The standard shown is for "Excellent."

| Criteria | "Excellent" Standard |
|---|---|
| **Safety** | Follows all safety rules, maintains a clean work area, returns materials to their proper place on completing a task. |
| **Initiative** | Stays on task, seeks additional work, offers assistance to peers, asks for help when needed, uses downtime effectively between jobs. |
| **Preparation** | On time, agenda present, writing instrument present, dressed to shop standards, awake and alert. |
| **Attitude** | Positive attitude, interacts appropriately with others, appropriate language, maintains self-control at all times. |
| **Work Ethic** | Work reflects best effort, cooperative, demonstrates peer leadership, self-starter, stays on task. |
| **Productivity** | Highest-quality work, actively looks for and suggests solutions, routinely uses time well, contributes useful ideas. |

## 4.3 Professional Documentation

Documentation is a non-negotiable professional skill. Students use three instruments.

### The Engineering Logbook

The primary daily record. Replaces the Engineering Notebook, retired in the 2025–26 year.

The logbook divides the day into **three intervals** — prior to break, break to lunch, and lunch to end of day. Each interval carries an EDP status code and records:

- **Work highlights and observations** — tasks completed, consultations, reviews, anomalies
- **Interval roadblocks** — specific technical failures and proposed workarounds
- **Visual evidence** — CAD renderings, logic diagrams, or photographic documentation, captioned

The day closes with:

- **Challenges and troubleshooting** — a summary of the day's technical failures and responses
- **Daily synthesis** — a technical summary analyzing decisions and evaluating progress against goals
- **Next class's agenda** — immediate objectives for the following session

**EDP status codes.** The logbook uses five codes as a working shorthand for the eight EDP stages. The compression is deliberate: a student choosing a code at the start of an interval shouldn't have to split hairs between adjacent planning stages.

| Code | Stage | Covers EDP stages |
|---|---|---|
| **[PI]** | Problem Identification / Research | Identify the Problem, Research & Brainstorm |
| **[DD]** | Detailed Design (CAD/Modeling) | Develop Solutions, Plan a Solution |
| **[FAB]** | Fabrication & Development | Create (Model or Prototype) |
| **[TE]** | Testing & Evaluation | Test and Evaluate |
| **[IR]** | Improve & Redesign | Improve and Redesign |

*Present Your Solution* has no daily code — it is a project milestone rather than a daily working state.

> **Standards note.** Standard **S3-e** requires students to *"maintain an engineering journal for projects to document design solutions."* The Engineering Logbook is this program's implementation of that requirement. The name differs from the state's wording; the function is the same.

### "Do Now!" Reflection

Quick self-assessment following a short skill-based activity. Records the title, equipment used, new knowledge acquired, a brief process summary, how the skill applies to current project work, and a confidence self-rating.

### Independent Focus Reflection

Formal term-end self-assessment of self-directed pathway work. Records the project title, equipment used, tasks completed, skills learned or improved, project status and progress, challenges and adaptations, an evaluation of overall project management, and goals for the next term.

# 5.0 Curriculum Resources

## 5.1 Core Textbooks

- ***Fundamentals of Modern Drafting*** (1st Ed.) — Paul Ross Wallach. Technical drawing, sketching, and CAD; ASME standards and descriptive geometry.
- ***Engineering Drawing and Design*** (3rd Ed.) — David A. Madsen. Visualization and CAD technology aligned to ANSI/ASME standards.
- ***Digital Electronics: Principles and Applications*** (6th Ed.) — Roger L. Tokheim. TTL/CMOS devices, troubleshooting, microprocessors.
- ***Foundations of Engineering*** (2nd Ed.) — Mark Holtzapple & W. Reece. Thermodynamics, Newton's laws, and professional skills, organized around the book's unifying concept of **Engineering Accounting** — systematically accounting for the physical and mathematical basis of every engineering decision. Where that term appears elsewhere in program documents, this is its source.
- ***Engineering Fundamentals: Design, Principles, and Careers*** (2nd Ed.) — Ryan A. Brown et al. Introduction to ten major engineering disciplines with career and education requirements.

## 5.2 Industry Platforms and E-Learning

- **Festo Learning Experience (Festo LX)** — e-learning combined with practical tasks for high-tech industry.
- **Arduino Education** — skill-oriented STEAM hardware and software for high school and university.
- **Universal Robots Academy** — hands-on kits and curriculum for collaborative robotics.
- **VEX Robotics** — STEM Labs as plugin lessons for collaborative, exploratory learning.
- **Autodesk Learning** — self-paced courses toward certification in industry-standard software.
- **Khan Academy** — mastery-based math and science tutorials supporting engineering calculation.
- **Brilliant.org** — interactive problem-solving in logic, science, and computer science.

## 5.3 Supplemental

- **Curated digital content** — vetted videos, professional articles, academic journals, and technical websites.

# 6.0 Appendix: Operational Documents

## 6.1 Makerspace Safety Rules

**Report all injuries**

- Report all injuries to an instructor immediately.
- Do not attempt to remove foreign objects from the eye or body.
- If chemicals get in the eyes, wash for 15 minutes before seeking medical treatment.

**Use protective gear**

- Wear eye protection — safety glasses with side shields, goggles, or face shields — at all times.
- No loose clothing, ties, jewelry, or gloves around moving machinery.
- Tie back or cover long hair.
- Wear only shoes that cover the entire foot.
- Wear suitable gloves when handling hot, sharp, or glass items.

**Prepare**

- Safety is the top priority. If unsure, ask.
- Know the locations of all first aid, fire, and safety equipment.
- Never use a tool unless you have been trained to use it safely.
- Never work alone when using power tools.
- Do not work in the shop if tired or in a hurry.
- No horseplay, and do not distract anyone using a tool.

**Use tools correctly**

- Use tools only for their designed purpose.
- Report broken tools or machines immediately.
- Do not remove tools from the room.
- Never walk away from a tool that is still running.
- Operate machines only with all required guards and shields in place.

**Clean up**

- Clean up every time you leave an area, including sweeping the floor.
- Clean and return all tools to their proper storage location.
- Use compressed air sparingly and never aim it at a person.
- Shut off and unplug machines when cleaning or repairing them.
- Keep floors around machines clean, dry, and free of trip hazards.

## 6.2 Classroom Rules and Expectations

1. Cell phones during break and lunch only.
2. Appropriate uniform, attire, and PPE are required and count toward the weekly grade.
3. No eating or drinking in shop common areas.
4. Respect your instructors and classmates — their property, personal space, and creative ideas.
5. Engineering Lab equipment is used only with permission. Do not intentionally misuse equipment or property.
6. Return items to their proper places after use, and help keep the workspace clean.
7. Stay in your assigned area. Do not leave the shop without verbal permission or a hall pass.
8. Computers are for educational purposes only.
9. Be on time for class and for work deadlines.
10. If you have questions or concerns, it is your responsibility to ask before an assignment is due.
11. Always work to your potential, and prepare to be challenged.
