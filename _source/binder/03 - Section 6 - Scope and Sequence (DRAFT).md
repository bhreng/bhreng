# 1.0 Program Philosophy

The BHR Engineering Technology program is built for technically oriented students heading to competitive technical colleges and universities. It combines state and national frameworks with a level of rigor beyond standard vocational expectations.

Its central strength is the dual-specialization model. Students get expert mentorship across the whole engineering lifecycle: **Mr. Frank (EDF)** for turning ideas into physical things — the visual, structural, and manufacturable side — and **Mr. Dryer (ESEC)** for the intelligence, functionality, and control that make systems work.

| Instructor | Specialization | Core focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Mr. Frank** | Engineering Design & Fabrication (EDF) | Transforming ideas into tangible forms — the visual, structural, and manufacturable aspects of engineering. |
| **Mr. Dryer** | Engineering Systems & Emerging Concepts (ESEC) | The intelligence, functionality, and control that drive modern engineered systems — digital, electrical, and analytical foundations. |

## 1.1 How the framework fits together

Two structures organize everything students do. The **five foundational pillars** describe *how* students learn — knowledge consumption, applied learning, safety and operations, professional practice, assessment and reflection. The **ten Program Content Categories** describe *what* they learn. Every activity sits somewhere on both.

## 1.2 The shape of a year

Every year follows the same arc. It opens with review and shop acclimation, builds through specialized technical units, and closes with a major project or capstone that integrates the year's learning.

**Grades 9, 10 and 11 each run eight units. Grade 12 runs five** — not because the year is lighter, but because the 35-day Senior Capstone occupies Terms 3 and 4. (The website's grade pages list only the EDF units from the *EDF Unit Breakdown* documents — six for Grade 9 — so they show a subset of the eight here; the ESEC units are Mr. Dryer's and run alongside.)

# 2.0 Grade 9 — Engineering I: Foundations and Acclimation

The freshman year establishes the base: the shop environment, the Engineering Design Process, parallel competence in physical and digital work, and an unwavering habit of safe practice.

**Emphasis.** On the EDF side, students learn professional CAD software and the first steps of engineering methodology. On the ESEC side, they meet measurement, mechanical principles, and basic logic circuits. A mandatory introduction to safe shop operations under the **Safety & Operations** pillar ensures every student can work in the shop competently before major project work begins.

| # | Unit | PCC Focus | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exploratory & Shop Readiness | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Shop acclimation, safety protocols, proper tool usage, before project work begins. |
| 2 | Intro to Digital Design & CAD | 1 · Digital Design & Visualization *(EDF)* | Parametric modeling and conceptual sketching. |
| 3 | Engineering Design & Planning | 9 · Engineering Methodology & Planning *(Shared)* | The Engineering Design Process, conceptual design, and design briefs. |
| 4 | Measurement & Mechanical Foundations | 5 · Foundational Science & Math *(ESEC)* | Technical measurement and applied math for engineering decisions. |
| 5 | Intro to Logic and Circuits | 7 · Digital Systems & Control *(ESEC)* | Basic circuit building and fundamental electronic principles. |
| 6 | Graphics & Documentation Basics | 2 · Technical Graphics & Documentation *(EDF)* | Reading drawings, understanding dimensions, and maintaining the Engineering Logbook. |
| 7 | Intro to Fabrication & Prototyping | 3 · Manufacturing & Fabrication Processes *(EDF)* | Safe operation of fabrication equipment, focused on 3D printing and additive manufacturing. |
| 8 | Grade 9 End-of-Year Project | 1 · Digital Design & Visualization *(EDF)* · 9 · Engineering Methodology & Planning *(Shared)* | Applying the design process and foundational skills to an integrated problem. |

# 3.0 Grade 10 — Engineering II: Technical Mastery and Safety

Sophomore year pivots from foundational knowledge to technical mastery. Professional-grade documentation and industry-recognized safety certification are the prerequisites for the specialization that follows in Grade 11. This is the year precision becomes the standard.

**Emphasis.** Technical communication (EDF) dominates — students produce professional drawings and specifications, a direct application of the **Professional Practice** pillar. Alongside it, empirical analysis (ESEC) teaches them to validate designs with data from systematic testing. The mandatory OSHA training is a genuine career credential and reinforces **Safety & Operations** as non-negotiable professional practice.

| # | Unit | PCC Focus | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review, Safety Certification & Prep | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Re-acclimation, project management review, preparation for higher expectations. |
| 2 | Skill Reinforcement: Technical Documentation Mastery | 2 · Technical Graphics & Documentation *(EDF)* | Professional drafting, including GD&T and technical reporting. |
| 3 | Electrical & Analog Systems | 5 · Foundational Science & Math *(ESEC)* | Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws applied to analyze and test analog circuits. |
| 4 | Safety & Certification (OSHA Prep) | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Dedicated OSHA training — a mandatory credential at this grade level. |
| 5 | Mechanical Principles & Simulation Intro | 5 · Foundational Science & Math *(ESEC)* · 8 · Empirical Analysis & Optimization *(ESEC)* | Static forces and Finite Element Analysis theory for predictive performance. |
| 6 | Intro to Quality Assurance & Testing | 4 · Quality Assurance & Systems Testing *(EDF)* | Inspection and calibration methodologies to verify products meet specification. |
| 7 | Civil/Architectural Foundations | 1 · Digital Design & Visualization *(EDF)* | Architectural drawing in BIM/Revit, structural systems, design coordination. |
| 8 | Grade 10 End-of-Year Capstone | 1 · Digital Design *(EDF)* · 2 · Technical Graphics *(EDF)* · 9 · Methodology & Planning *(Shared)* | A complex design-build challenge integrating professional documentation and data analysis. |

# 4.0 Grade 11 — Engineering III: Specialization and Industrial Application

Junior year is where the two specializations converge. Students apply a consolidated skill set to industry-relevant problems, integrating digital intelligence with physical systems, and finish with a capstone demanding creativity, technical integration, and sound engineering logic.

**Emphasis.** Advanced Manufacturing and DFM theory (EDF) teaches students to design parts that can actually be built efficiently. Control Systems, Mechatronics, and Algorithmic Programming (ESEC) give them the means to make those parts intelligent. Professional project management ties the two together — a key application of the **Professional Practice** pillar.

| # | Unit | PCC Focus | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review, Acclimation & Higher Expectations Prep | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Return to the shop and transition to junior-year expectations. |
| 2 | Roles of an Engineer & Professional Management | 10 · Professionalism *(Shared)* · 9 · Methodology & Planning *(Shared)* | Professional engineering roles and project management with tools such as Gantt charts. |
| 3 | Skill Reinforcement for Upperclassmen | 1, 2, 3, 4 *(EDF synthesis)* | Flexible unit targeting underdeveloped skills — advanced CAD, structural analysis — needed for the capstone. |
| 4 | Advanced Manufacturing & DFM Theory | 3 · Manufacturing & Fabrication Processes *(EDF)* | Design for Manufacturing theory and the practice of additive and subtractive fabrication. |
| 5 | Control Systems & Mechatronics | 7 · Digital Systems & Control *(ESEC)* | Control systems theory and the integration of sensors and actuators into working mechatronic systems. |
| 6 | Algorithmic Programming & Analysis | 6 · Software & Algorithmic Engineering *(ESEC)* · 8 · Empirical Analysis *(ESEC)* | Writing code to control systems, and advanced simulation for performance analysis. |
| 7 | Junior Capstone: Architectural Design | 1, 2, 9, 10 *(synthesis)* | 15-day project: conceptual and detailed design of original architectural concepts. |
| 8 | Year-End Wrap-Up & Portfolio Prep | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Organizing work, year-end reports, reflection on skill development for the portfolio. |

## 4.1 The Junior Capstone: A Dual Architectural Challenge

A 15-day open-ended project testing whether students can balance form against function. Students take on **two** distinct challenges — one small in scale, one large.

**Project One — Unique Concept.** Develop an original concept with a distinctive structural or visual aspect: an upside-down building, a structure with integrated gardens, something that has not been built the ordinary way. The requirement that makes it engineering rather than art is validating the concept's engineering logic through research or testing.

**Project Two — Revitalization.** Select an existing area, space, or structure and plan how to bring new life to it. Students research comparable revitalization efforts, develop a vision for how people would connect with the space, and pitch it.

# 5.0 Grade 12 — Engineering IV: Professionalism and Capstone Mastery

Senior year is the culmination of the program: professional-level mastery, leadership, and independence, applied to an original self-directed project that synthesizes design, fabrication, systems, and analysis.

**Emphasis.** Seniors demonstrate mastery of quality assurance (EDF) and systems testing (ESEC), verifying performance against industry-level standards. They exercise leadership through advanced application of the **Professional Practice** and **Safety & Operations** pillars — mentoring younger students and taking on project management roles. The year ends with a comprehensive portfolio and its defense.

| # | Unit | PCC Focus | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review, Career Readiness & Leadership | 10 · Professionalism & Safe Operations *(Shared)* | Acclimation plus employability, entrepreneurship, and senior-level expectations. |
| 2 | Skill Reinforcement: Shop Equipment Mastery | 3 · Manufacturing & Fabrication *(EDF)* · 10 · Professionalism *(Shared)* | Open-ended work demonstrating mastery of main shop equipment, additive and subtractive. |
| 3 | Skill Reinforcement / Elective Exploration | Standard 12 · Advanced Elective Area *(Shared)* | Flexible unit for capstone skill-building or Independent Focus work in an elective engineering area. |
| 4 | Robotics and Automation | 7 · Digital Systems & Control *(ESEC)* · 9 · Methodology & Planning *(Shared)* | Advanced robotics principles, automation systems, intelligent systems integration. |
| 5 | Advanced Mastery & Senior Capstone | 1–10 *(full integration)* | 35-day project: research, design, and development of an original solution. |

## 5.1 The Senior Capstone: An Original Engineering Solution

A **35-day** open-ended project, running through Terms 3 and 4 — the final showcase of everything a student has learned. Not a guided assignment: students identify their own problem, develop a solution, and manage the whole process from concept to completion.

**Project management.** Maintain meticulous engineering documentation, daily logbook entries, and records of instructor meetings from start to finish.

**Research.** Define and justify a problem, research prior solutions, consider multiple approaches, and evaluate them with tools such as a decision matrix.

**Design.** Move from conceptual ideas to detailed designs, analyzing viability, material choices, and production methods.

**Prototype, test, redesign.** Build and test a prototype, collect and analyze data, and iterate.

**Reflection and presentation.** Showcase the final work, defend the process, and explain the critical decisions made along the way in a formal presentation.

# 6.0 The Four-Year Trajectory

The program moves students deliberately from foundational knowledge, through specialized application, to professional-level mastery. Each year builds on the last. Graduates are technically proficient, but more importantly they are independent problem-solvers fluent in both the physical and digital sides of engineering.

The dual EDF/ESEC structure is what makes that possible: students come out understanding both the physical *form* of a product and the digital *intelligence* that drives its function.

By graduation students hold transferable competencies in project leadership, technical communication, and ethical practice — the things competitive post-secondary engineering programs and modern employers actually look for.
