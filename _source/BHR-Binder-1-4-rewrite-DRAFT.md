# Section 1 — Instructor Specialization Overview

The program is taught by two instructors with complementary specializations: **Mr. Frank (EDF — Engineering Design & Fabrication)** and **Mr. Dryer (ESEC — Engineering Systems & Emerging Concepts)**. Between them they cover the full engineering lifecycle, from concept and physical fabrication through systems integration and analysis.

Both instructors support all student projects. The split exists so students know who to go to for what.

## 1.1 Mr. Frank — Engineering Design & Fabrication (EDF)

**Focus:** turning ideas into physical things. The visual, structural, and manufacturable side of engineering.

**Expertise**

- **Industrial and aesthetic design** — user-centered design, form, ergonomics, visual impact.
- **Technical graphics and documentation** — CAD modeling, drafting standards including GD&T, architectural drawing, structural representation, as-built documentation. *(Standard 2)*
- **Prototyping and manufacturing** — Design for Manufacturing for both additive processes (3D printing) and subtractive ones (laser engraving, CNC machining). *(Standard 7)*
- **Structural and construction principles** — structural integrity, construction material properties, civil engineering concepts. *(Standard 10)*

**Come to Mr. Frank for**

- Developing designs in CAD, for looks and for function
- Preparing parts for 3D printing or laser cutting
- Building structures that hold up and meet code
- Producing professional drawings and documentation
- Working through physical prototype iterations

**EDP stages emphasized:** Develop Solutions (form), Plan a Solution (physical build), Create (fabrication), Improve & Redesign (physical iteration), Present Your Solution (visual communication).

**Career paths:** product design, industrial design, architectural engineering, design-focused mechanical engineering, manufacturing engineering, construction management, technical drafting.

## 1.2 Mr. Dryer — Engineering Systems & Emerging Concepts (ESEC)

**Focus:** the intelligence, functionality, and control that make engineered systems work. The digital, electrical, and analytical side.

**Expertise**

- **Digital systems and controls** — electrical fundamentals, digital electronics, logic design, programming (Arduino, Python, PLC), control systems, mechatronics. *(Standards 5, 6, 9)*
- **Software and algorithmic analysis** — efficient code, software architecture, computational thinking applied to engineering problems. *(Standard 6)*
- **Applied research and emerging technology** — AI and machine learning in control systems, IoT principles, quantum computing concepts, identifying where the field is heading.
- **System analysis and mechanical function** — materials science for functional application, Finite Element Analysis theory, kinematics, dynamics, actuation. *(Standards 7, 8)*

**Come to Mr. Dryer for**

- Designing or troubleshooting circuits and digital logic
- Writing and debugging code for microcontrollers and robots
- Analyzing system performance with data or simulation
- Integrating sensors, actuators, and controls into a working system
- The theory behind newer technologies

**EDP stages emphasized:** Identify the Problem (functional requirements), Research & Brainstorm (technology options), Develop Solutions (system architecture, code), Create (functional prototypes), Test & Evaluate (performance metrics), Improve & Redesign (system optimization).

**Career paths:** electrical engineering, software engineering, computer engineering, robotics, control systems, systems integration, R&D, automation engineering.

# Section 2 — Foundational Pillars

Five pillars organize everything students do, from the daily Do Now to the capstone. Each pillar answers a different question: what are you learning, what are you building, are you working safely, are you working professionally, and how do you know it worked.

## 2.1 Knowledge Consumption (KC)

**What it is:** acquiring new information, concepts, and procedural skills — the "why" and the "how-to" before application.

**Activities:** Do Now!, Theory-Based Lesson, Training-Based Lesson.

**By specialization**

- **EDF:** industrial design principles, drafting standards and GD&T, DFM theory for additive and subtractive geometry, CAD technique, 3D printing and laser engraving theory.
- **ESEC:** digital electronics, logic design, algorithmic analysis, coding in Arduino/Python/PLC, systems engineering, mechatronics, electrical fundamentals, sensor theory, materials science, FEA theory.

**Engineering roles:** primarily Research Engineer; aspects of Design and Development Engineer when learning new tools.

**EDP stages:** Identify the Problem, Research & Brainstorm.

**Standards:** *3 (Fundamentals of Engineering), 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 depending on content; 15 (Digital Literacy) for software proficiency.*

## 2.2 Applied Learning (AL)

**What it is:** using what you've learned to solve problems, build designs, develop systems, and produce something real. The doing.

**Activities:** Project Work, Assignment, Lab & Testing, Independent Study.

**By specialization:** the student's **assigned instructor** guides applied work, drawing on KC from both EDF and ESEC as the project requires.

**Engineering roles:** Design Engineer, Development Engineer, Production & Construction Engineer.

**EDP stages:** Develop Solutions, Plan a Solution, Create, Test & Evaluate, Improve & Redesign.

**Standards:** *3, 11 (Integrated Area Research), 12 (Advanced Elective Area), plus the technical standard matching the project's discipline.*

## 2.3 Safety & Operations (S&O)

**What it is:** keeping the shop secure, functional, and maintained. Equipment protocols, organization, stewardship.

**Activities:** Training-Based Lesson (safety), Shop Stewardship, and continuous adherence during all hands-on work.

**By specialization**

- **EDF:** 3D printer operation, laser engraver safety, manual fabrication tools, digital file management.
- **ESEC:** power tools, electrical safety, machine tool operation including CNC and mill, tool maintenance.

**Engineering roles:** Production & Construction Engineer, Operations Engineer.

**EDP stages:** Plan a Solution (safety considerations), Create (safe execution).

**Standards:** *1 (Safety and Health) — including PPE, Lockout/Tagout, and SDS use.*

## 2.4 Professional Practice (PP)

**What it is:** the non-technical skills every engineering career needs — teamwork, communication, ethics, and project management.

**Activities:** Professional Development Activities, One-on-One Meeting / Mentoring, and collaboration integrated into project work.

**By specialization**

- **EDF:** design critique, visual communication and presentation, user-centered design, design management.
- **ESEC:** technical documentation, systematic troubleshooting, requirements analysis, technical reporting.

**Engineering roles:** Management Engineer, Sales Engineer, Operations Engineer — and every role, for ethics and teamwork.

**EDP stages:** Identify the Problem (user needs), Research & Brainstorm (collaboration), Plan a Solution (team planning), Present Your Solution.

**Standards:** *2 (Role of Professionals), 13 (Employability Skills), 14 (Entrepreneurship), 4 (Project Management).*

## 2.5 Assessment & Reflection (A&R)

**What it is:** evaluating outcomes, documenting progress, and thinking about your own thinking. Accountability and improvement.

**Activities:** Project Presentation / Review, Quiz / Test, Project Reflections, Do Now! reflection, Engineering Logbook.

**By specialization:** the assigned instructor grades and gives feedback, drawing on both specializations.

- **EDF evaluates:** design quality, drafting accuracy, evidence of iteration, fabrication quality.
- **ESEC evaluates:** functional success, analytical rigor, code efficiency, system performance, adherence to spec.

**Engineering roles:** all roles document and evaluate; especially Research Engineer, Design and Development Engineer, Management Engineer.

**EDP stages:** Test & Evaluate, Present Your Solution.

**Standards:** *3, 11, 12 — documentation requirements; 13 for professional communication of results.*

# Section 3 — Program Content Categories

Ten categories describing what the program teaches. Each names its scope, what falls inside it, and which instructor leads it.

### 1. Digital Design & Visualization
**Scope:** industry-standard CAD software and creative technique — sketching, industrial design — used to conceive, develop, and present spatial ideas as functional digital models and animations.
**Scope includes:** CAD, sketching, industrial design, parametric modeling, direct modeling, generative design for form, animation.
**Lead:** EDF · **Standard:** *8*

### 2. Technical Graphics & Documentation
**Scope:** creating and reading the formal language of engineering drawings — dimensioning, tolerancing, standardized reporting — so components can be manufactured and assembled correctly.
**Scope includes:** drafting, GD&T, technical reporting, the Engineering Logbook, BIM, architectural plans, part lists.
**Lead:** EDF · **Standard:** *2*

### 3. Manufacturing & Fabrication Processes
**Scope:** selecting materials and using shop equipment, traditional and advanced, to turn digital designs into physical products — from rapid prototyping through assembly and structural fabrication.
**Scope includes:** additive manufacturing, rapid prototyping, CAM, assembly, construction, material joining, structural fabrication.
**Lead:** EDF · **Standard:** *7*

### 4. Quality Assurance & Systems Testing
**Scope:** verifying that a product meets every design requirement, not just that it functions — inspection, calibration, and data collection in service of reliability.
**Scope includes:** testing, quality control, inspection, calibration, statistical process control, final product verification.
**Lead:** EDF · **Standard:** *4*

### 5. Foundational Science & Math
**Scope:** the physics, chemistry, and mathematics behind engineering decisions — the basis for calculation and for understanding how materials and structures behave.
**Scope includes:** physics, mathematics, material properties, structural theory, electrical fundamentals.
**Lead:** ESEC · **Standard:** *3, 5*

### 6. Software & Algorithmic Engineering
**Scope:** developing the logic, structure, and code that let systems operate autonomously, process information, and run automated tasks. Emphasis on debugging and logic analysis.
**Scope includes:** coding, programming, firmware development, software architecture, debugging, logic analysis.
**Lead:** ESEC · **Standard:** *6*

### 7. Digital Systems & Control
**Scope:** the hardware side of automation — designing, connecting, and programming microcontrollers, sensors, and digital circuits to control mechanical and electro-mechanical processes.
**Scope includes:** automation, digital electronics, microcontrollers, control systems, mechatronics.
**Lead:** ESEC · **Standard:** *9*

### 8. Empirical Analysis & Optimization
**Scope:** using computational methods such as FEA, together with real test data, to simulate performance, find weaknesses, and refine a design toward optimal function.
**Scope includes:** functional analysis, FEA and CFD simulation, optimization, generative design for performance, data-driven testing.
**Lead:** ESEC · **Standard:** *8*

### 9. Engineering Methodology & Planning
**Scope:** the structured processes for managing a project start to finish — defining the problem through the EDP, then planning resources with Gantt charts and work breakdown structures.
**Scope includes:** project management, the Engineering Design Process, WBS, decision matrices, Gantt scheduling.
**Lead:** Shared · **Standard:** *4*

### 10. Professionalism & Safe Operations
**Scope:** career-ready skills, ethical responsibility, working in team roles, and rigorous adherence to safety and maintenance protocols in the lab and on site.
**Scope includes:** engineering roles, employability skills, technical communication, shop safety, tool usage, OSHA protocols, maintenance.
**Lead:** Shared · **Standard:** *1, 13*

> **Note on overlap.** PCC 9 corresponds closely to Standard 4, and PCC 10 to Standards 1 and 13 plus the S&O pillar. If the PCCs are ever consolidated, these two are the first candidates to fold into the standards directly.

# Section 4 — Core Class Tasks & Their Classification

The activity types students encounter, and how each maps to the pillars. This list governs how work is categorized.

### 1. Do Now!
Short focused activity at the start of class — a micro-skill, a key concept, or a brief application problem.
**Primary:** Knowledge Consumption · **Secondary:** A&R, when it includes a quick reflection
**Specialization:** matches whatever KC is being delivered that morning.

### 2. Theory-Based Lesson
Study of scientific principles, engineering concepts, analytical methods, or historical context. Comprehension and the "why."
**Primary:** Knowledge Consumption
**Specialization:** EDF — structural principles, DFM theory. ESEC — electrical fundamentals, control algorithms, materials science.

### 3. Training-Based Lesson
Practice aimed at a specific procedural skill, software proficiency, equipment operation, or safety protocol. The "how."
**Primary:** Knowledge Consumption when learning the skill; **Safety & Operations** when learning equipment safety or setup.
**Specialization:** EDF — advanced CAD, 3D printer operation. ESEC — programming syntax, circuit building, multimeter use.

### 4. Project Work
Extended multi-day work applying a range of skills to design, develop, test, and produce something real. Where KC becomes AL.
**Primary:** Applied Learning · **Secondary:** S&O (safe tool use), PP (teamwork, communication), A&R (daily reflection)
**Specialization:** the assigned instructor guides it, drawing on both EDF and ESEC as needed.

### 5. Assignment
A focused task — problem set, design brief response, research report, small design challenge — serving as a checkpoint. Preparatory or standalone.
**Primary:** usually Applied Learning; sometimes Knowledge Consumption (a research summary) or Professional Practice (a technical report)
**Specialization:** reflects the assigned instructor's focus or the current KC.

### 6. Lab & Testing
Hands-on experimentation, data collection, functional testing, troubleshooting, verifying theory.
**Primary:** Applied Learning · **Secondary:** A&R (analysis, conclusions), S&O (lab safety)
**Specialization:** material stress tests on a printed part (EDF); circuit performance, sensor calibration, robot movement verification (ESEC).

### 7. Independent Focus
Self-directed, term-length work in a chosen emerging engineering field, for upper-year students. Research, design, or development, demonstrating initiative and comprehensive skill.
**Primary:** Applied Learning · **Secondary:** KC (self-directed research), PP (project management, self-motivation), A&R (reflections, logbook)
**Specialization:** students draw on Knowledge Consumption from both instructors; the assigned instructor oversees the applied work and assessment.
**Replaces:** Independent Study, retired terminology.
**Pathways:** Industrial Design · Architecture/Civil · Mechanical · Software · Electrical · Automation/Robotics · Project Management
**Standard:** *12 (Advanced Elective Area)*

### 8. One-on-One Meeting / Mentoring
Individual discussion for guidance, project feedback, career advice, or specific learning needs.
**Primary:** Professional Practice (career and project advice) or Assessment & Reflection (feedback and progress)
**Secondary:** KC (clarifying a concept) or AL (troubleshooting)

### 9. Quiz / Test
Formal assessment of comprehension or recall of procedural knowledge.
**Primary:** Assessment & Reflection
**Specialization:** reflects KC delivered by either instructor, or general AL outcomes.

### 10. Shop Stewardship ⚠ NEW
Maintaining the shop — tool organization, equipment cleaning and calibration, workspace restoration.
**Primary:** Safety & Operations
**Specialization:** EDF — fabrication tools, printer and laser maintenance, digital file organization. ESEC — power tools, machine tools, electrical equipment.
**Standard:** *1*

### 11. Professional Development Activity (PDA) ⚠ NEW
Workshop-format sessions on teamwork, communication strategy, ethics, or project management methodology.
**Primary:** Professional Practice
**Specialization:** EDF — design critique, presentation. ESEC — technical documentation, requirements analysis.
**Standard:** *2, 13, 14*

### 12. Project Presentation / Review ⚠ NEW
Formal or informal showcase of work, explaining process and receiving feedback.
**Primary:** Assessment & Reflection · **Secondary:** PP (technical communication)
**Specialization:** EDF evaluates design quality and visual communication; ESEC evaluates functional success and analytical rigor.
**Standard:** *13*

### 13. Project Reflection ⚠ NEW
Mid-point and end-of-project self-assessment, including Independent Study reflections.
**Primary:** Assessment & Reflection
**Standard:** *3, 12*

### 14. Engineering Logbook ⚠ NEW
Daily documentation of progress, problems encountered, and solutions attempted. Completed in three intervals across the day — prior to break, break to lunch, lunch to end of day — each carrying an EDP status code, plus an end-of-day synthesis and the agenda for next class.
**Primary:** Assessment & Reflection · **Secondary:** PP (professional documentation practice)
**Replaces:** the Engineering Notebook, retired this past year.
**Note:** the crosswalk names the Engineering Daily Logbook as the documentation requirement for Standard 3, which makes this the most consequential of the five omissions — the program's core documentation instrument had no entry in the document that classifies work.
**Standard:** *3*
