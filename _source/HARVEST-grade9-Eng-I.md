## EOY Project — Urban planning and design

**Objective.** To design a section of a city using a variety of
industry-standard software tools, integrating 2D mapping, 3D conceptual design
and graphic enhancement. Students develop skills in AutoCAD, Fusion 360 /
SketchUp and CorelDraw while applying principles of urban planning and design.

Students work individually to design a section of a city, focusing on the area
around public transit stops. Designing an entire city is not required, but **a
minimum of four distinct transit stop areas** must be developed, each with at
least one featured building or attraction designed in detail.

**2D site map (AutoCAD)** — the layout of the four transit stop areas,
accurately representing streets and intersections, transit lines and stop
locations, basic building footprints, green spaces and public areas, and scale
and dimensions.

**3D building conceptual design (Fusion 360 or SketchUp)** — select a featured
building or attraction from each transit stop area and model it, focusing on
building size, basic architectural features, and relationship to the
surrounding area.

**3D area conceptual design** — choose one transit stop area and model the
broader area, integrating the featured buildings, showing the relationship
between buildings, streets and public spaces, and conveying the overall
character and layout.

**Enhanced map presentation (CorelDraw)** — import the 2D map from AutoCAD and
enhance it with colour, textures and patterns; labels, legends and symbols; to
produce a visually appealing and informative representation.

**Documentation.** A design notebook with initial concept sketches for each
transit stop area, sketches and diagrams for featured buildings, and notes on
design decisions and iterations. Daily journals covering work completed,
challenges and solutions, and next steps. A presentation showcasing the 2D map,
3D models and enhanced map.

## Software Workflow Evaluation Report: 3ds Max vs. Maya

A three-day exercise: independently explore two industry-standard 3D modelling
programs and assess their fit for the shop's workflow, ending in a comparative
analysis and a demonstration through an original design.

**Objectives** — rapid software assessment of interface, core tools and
workflows; workflow compatibility with existing design and production
processes; a strategic comparison of strengths, weaknesses and optimal use
cases against the shop's needs (product visualisation, architectural rendering,
character development); practical application through an original design;
professional reporting.

**Day 1 — 3ds Max.** Use online resources to explore the environment. Focus on
viewport navigation and manipulation, creating and modifying basic geometric
primitives, fundamental transforms (move, rotate, scale), and common modifiers
such as Extrude and Bevel. *Deliverable:* a simple non-organic object — a basic
furniture piece, a structural component, a stylised architectural element —
with screenshots of the work and key interface elements.

**Day 2 — Maya.** Repeat Day 1. The object may be a direct replication of the
Day 1 object for a clearer comparison, or one of similar complexity,
potentially exploring basic organic forms.

**Day 3 — strategic application and recommendation.** Select the program best
suited for a specific type of work and justify it.

## Time Management Challenge: Dual Projects

Demonstrate AutoCAD skills across two distinct design scales while managing
your time. **Three days for both parts.**

**Part 1 — Moonbase layout (large scale).** A 2D layout focused on overall
organisation: living quarters, research labs, power generation, landing and
launch areas, resource extraction or storage, recreation areas. Logical
organisation, key areas labelled with text, focus on relationships between
zones and overall flow. Accurate dimensions where appropriate, but the emphasis
is macro-level.

**Part 2 — Grocery store floorplan (small scale).** Detailed layout of a large
store (Whole Foods, Shaws) or a small one (Trader Joe's). Include the overall
shape, shelving and aisle layouts, detailed placement of key areas (produce,
dairy, checkout), and text labels for all areas. **Accurate dimensions are
crucial for this part.**

*Optional if time allows:* the front facade, colour via hatching, the parking
lot and surrounding area.

The challenge is allocating time: the moonbase needs a broad conceptual
approach, the grocery store demands detail and accuracy.

## Take-Apart Toy Car in Fusion 360

**Objective** — accurately recreate a take-apart toy car; develop proficiency
with measurement tools, individual part files and multi-part assembly; produce
orthographic drawings, renders and exploded animations; create a basic parts
list.

This is reverse engineering a physical toy: measure and analyse each part and
recreate it as a separate component, then assemble.

**Disassembly and measurement** — carefully disassemble; use calipers, rulers
and other tools to measure each part accurately; take detailed notes and
sketches including shape, features and intricate details.

**Individual part modelling** — a separate Fusion file for each part, modelled
from the measurement data, paying close attention to tolerances and fit.

**Assembly** — a new assembly file, import all parts, use joints and
constraints to replicate the physical toy's functionality.

**Orthographic drawings** — top, front, side and isometric views, with
dimensions and annotations.

**Renders** — high quality, experimenting with materials, lighting and camera
angles.

**Part list** — part name, quantity, material, description.

***Bonus*** — an exploded animation showing how the parts fit together.

*Attached: Toy Pull Apart Car Project — Part List (Google Sheets).*

## Engineering Shop Redesign

Your team will analyse the current layout of the engineering shop — the two
computer labs/classrooms and the makerspace — measure all existing spaces and
furniture, create accurate 2D floor plans in AutoCAD, then propose a redesigned
layout in Fusion 360 that better serves the program. **One change that must be
implemented is eliminating the computers from one classroom.** You will design
and model all the needed furniture in Fusion 360.

**Measurements** — length and width of each room including the storage closet
and restroom, using measuring tapes; dimensions of all furniture (desks,
tables, cabinets) and their current placement; location and dimensions of
doors, windows and fixed features (outlets, light switches, plumbing).

**Deliverables** — a 2D floor plan of the current layout drawn to scale in
AutoCAD with all measured dimensions, furniture placement and annotations for
doors, windows and fixed features; and a Fusion 360 3D model of the proposed
redesign including all proposed changes.

## Independent Study — Project 1

Independent study matters in this shop because it leads into Senior Capstone.
Each year builds on what you have learned and gives you an outlet to push
yourself in a direction of your choosing.

**Project 1** is about exploring options and brainstorming project ideas. It
can be a single document, a slideshow, or several documents.

**Brainstorming list** — search a list of broad engineering fields (mechanical,
electrical, robotics, software, biomedical, environmental) and list the ones
that interest you.

**Video exploration** — find engineering videos online (construction, bridges,
prosthetics, robots, software applications) and discuss the applications of
each field and their impact on our lives.

Then identify potential fields by exploring your chosen areas further: what
specific aspects intrigue you, and are there real-world problems within that
field you'd like to learn more about?

**Project idea generation** — generate a list of potential project ideas.
Design-based (create a new product or solution within your chosen field),
repair-based (fix, modify or improve an existing object), or research-based
(research a specific engineering challenge).

**Also** — share and discuss your ideas with classmates for feedback. This is a
starting point; ideas can be refined through the year as research and skills
develop. Think about feasibility and the challenges you might encounter.
