## What twenty-five shop days actually means

The term is not short. What is short is **continuity** — the gap. Whatever a
student is mid-way through goes cold for a week, every week, all year. That is
the constraint the structure has to be built around, not scarcity of hours.

Which changes the diagnosis:

- **A small build is back on the table.** Across five full shop days' worth of
  E.E.P. time there is room to make something, provided it can survive being
  put down for a week at a time.
- **The real risk is the restart, not the finish.** Five separate cold starts
  per term is where a self-directed project dies. Anything that cannot be
  picked back up from written notes in ten minutes will quietly stall.
- **Where the E.E.P. sits in the week is still the open question.** If it is a
  slice — a morning a shop week — that is five sessions a term. If it is a full
  day a shop week, that is five full days. Both are "five weeks"; they are very
  different programs. This is question 1 below and it is the one that decides
  the rest.

## Proposed shape — one page in, one page out, and the sessions between

### First session of the term — Declare
Half a page, ten minutes, done in the room.

- Which hub you are in this term.
- Why. "It's next to what I want to do" and "no idea, looked interesting" are
  both fine answers, and which one it is matters.
- One thing you want to be able to say by the end of the term.

Not a proposal, not approved, not graded. It exists so that the last session
has something to compare against.

### The middle of the term — one substantial thing
The student picks **one**, and the hub's five topics are the menu:

- **Look at the field properly.** Roles, what the work is actually like, what a
  job posting asks for, what a program you'd apply to expects. Comes back as
  something you could tell the class in two minutes.
- **Gain one skill you did not have.** A tutorial finished, a certification
  level reached, a technique practiced to the point where it works. Evidenced,
  not claimed.
- **Build one small thing.** Explicitly small. A part, a circuit, a script, a
  drawing set. Not a project — an artifact.

One of those three, at depth, beats three of them at a glance. If the E.E.P.
turns out to be a full day a shop week rather than a morning, this floor can go
up to two — but not three, because the fifth cold start is where the third one
gets abandoned.

**But: the field one should come round at least once a year.** It is the beat
that carries your whole paths-out-of-the-program goal, and it is the one that
loses every fight against something you can hold. Rather than requiring it
every term, require that **across a student's four terms in a year, at least
one is a look at the field.** Same coverage, none of the per-term weight.

### Last session of the term — Reflect and decide
The term-end reflection already exists in every hub's Get the Files list, so
the slot is there. Three questions, and the third is the one that matters:

- Was this what you thought it was?
- What would you tell someone picking this hub next term?
- Staying or moving? Either is a good answer — say why.

The weekly journal (`BHR-ENG-EEP-Weekly-Journal.docx`) runs underneath all of
it — one entry per shop week, so about five a term. That is light, and it is
also the thing that makes the week-off gap survivable. Its "Next shop week"
box is doing more work than it looks like it is: it is the note that turns a
cold restart into a warm one.

## The multi-term picture — this is where the value is

Even at twenty-five shop days, **no single term carries much weight next to the
pattern across eight of them.** The record is the main artifact; the term
reflections are what feed it.

**A one-page pathway record that follows the student.** One row per term —
hub, one line on what came of it, stayed or moved. **Six rows: Grade 11 Terms
1–4 and Grade 12 Terms 1–2**, because the E.E.P. ends when the Senior Capstone
starts. By then a student can see their own shape at a glance: *three terms
Architecture, one Project Management, one Electrical, one Industrial Design.*

A seventh row for the capstone is worth adding whether or not it grew out of an
E.E.P. — the record then shows the whole arc, including the case where the
capstone went somewhere none of the terms pointed.

That page is worth more in a college or apprenticeship interview than any
single five-week piece of work, because it shows a decision history. It also
makes "get rounded on purpose" concrete rather than advice.

I can build it as a one-page PDF the student keeps in the binder, or a Sheet if
you want it filled in digitally and visible to you.

## Answered — now on the site

**When it ends.** The E.E.P. runs until the Senior Capstone starts. An E.E.P.
project *may* become the capstone, but nothing requires the two to be
connected. **So the pathway record has six rows, not eight** — Grade 11 Terms
1–4, Grade 12 Terms 1–2.

**Terms connect, or don't.** A term can start something new or continue the
last one. Set a goal for the term, hit it, pick it up next term.

**Grading is not on the work.** Effort, participation, and meeting the
documentation requirements. Projects and assignments are where work gets
graded; the E.E.P. is where the freedom to explore lives, so the grade cannot
punish a project that went nowhere interesting. This is the single most
important thing to state plainly to students — it is what makes the risky pick
safe to take, and it is stated on the site now.

**Who does it.** Every student in the shop. Mr. Dryer will likely run it for
Grades 9 and 10 and may frame it his own way, so the site describes the
structure without claiming his version of it.

**The radio example — yours, and better than anything I wrote.** Term 1
Electrical for the circuit, Term 2 Industrial Design for the enclosure. Same
project, two hubs, because the hub follows the part of the problem you are on.
That is now a worked example on the pathways page. It does more work than four
paragraphs of explanation: it shows carryover, hub-switching and
getting-rounded all at once, and it makes clear that switching is not
dabbling.
