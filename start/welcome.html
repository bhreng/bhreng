Engineering Technology · Welcome

Welcome to the shop

Everything you need for your first week: who to ask for what, what to wear, how the shop runs, and how you are graded.

What this program is

You are joining a program that treats you like an engineer rather than a student who happens to be in a shop. You will design things, build them, test them, and explain why they worked or did not. Some of what you make will fail. That is part of it — what matters is that you can say why.

Who to ask for what

Both of us teach across all four years. Go to whoever fits the problem in front of you — you will not be sent away for asking the wrong one.

Mr. Frank · Grades 11 & 12 Helps you make it real
  • Designing in CAD so it works and looks right
  • Getting a part ready to 3D print or laser cut
  • Making a structure that holds up
  • Drawings someone else could build from
  • Fixing a prototype that came out wrong
Mr. Dryer · Grades 9 & 10 Helps you make it work
  • Designing or troubleshooting a circuit
  • Writing and debugging code for a board or robot
  • Working out why a system misbehaves
  • Getting sensors and motors working together
  • Showing with data that it does what you claim

What you can earn here

OSHA 10 — Construction
In Grade 10. The credential that lets you work on the equipment, and it is recognized well outside this building.
Autodesk Certified User
In Inventor, Fusion 360 and Revit Architecture. Which ones you go for depends on the work you are doing and when you are ready.
Cooperative education
Working in a real engineering setting for part of your schedule. Available to juniors from Term 3 and seniors from Term 1.

What to wear

You wear a uniform during your shop week. It is not about looking smart — it is about being dressed for a room with machines in it.

ItemWhat is required
Shop attireAnything carrying the Engineering Technology logo, bought through the school store — T-shirts long or short sleeve, hoodies, crew necks, quarter-zips, sweaters. Any of it counts.
FootwearShoes, sneakers or work boots. No open-toed shoes, sandals or Crocs — anywhere in the shop, every day.
Eye protectionRequired in the Makerspace. Situational in the main shop — if you are unsure, put them on.

Ordering. Uniforms are ordered at store.bluehills.org during the window posted on the website — that is when pricing is best and delivery to the school is free. There is no limit on how many you buy; most students get at least two. Refer to the Blue Hills Parent/Student Handbook for anything else about attire.

Safety, in short

The full rules are posted in the shop and you will be trained on them. These are the ones that matter before you touch anything.

  • Report every injury immediately, however small. If a chemical gets in your eyes, wash for 15 minutes before seeking treatment.
  • Never use a tool you have not been trained and authorized on.
  • Never work alone with power tools — two people, both visible to each other.
  • Machines run only with all guards and shields in place. Never walk away from a running tool.
  • Clean your area every time you leave it, including the floor.
