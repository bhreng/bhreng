Shop safety · General

Safe and appropriate technology use

Most shop safety is about not hurting yourself. This part is about not hurting anyone else — with a scanner, a camera, an account, or something you make and send out into the world.

What this covers

Scanning and photographing people
A 3D scan is a record of someone’s body and a photo is a record of their face. Both need asking first, and both need taking no for an answer.
Other people’s designs
Almost every model you download carries a licence saying what you may do with it. Read it, follow it, and never present someone else’s work as your own.
AI tools
Use them, say you used them, and check the output. A confident wrong answer is still wrong, and the engineer who signs it owns it.
Accounts and machines
A signed-in account is your name on whatever happens next. Sign out. And never bypass a safety feature in software — that is the same act as taping a switch down, with less evidence.
What gets made
If you are not sure something should be made here, ask before you start rather than after it exists.

Check yourself

Scanning, cameras, AI, licences and accounts — the part of shop practice that affects other people. Every question has to be right before you finish — a wrong answer tells you why, and you pick again. There is no score and no time limit.

This check is not a record. Nothing you do here is sent anywhere or stored. When you finish, screenshot the completion panel and attach it to the acknowledgement in Google Classroom — that is what counts.
