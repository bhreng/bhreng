3D printer certification

How much you can do on a printer on your own depends on how much training you have done on that machine. This page is the whole rule.

Every print job is approved by an instructor first — at every level, on every machine, no exceptions. Certification changes who has to stand next to you, not whether you need approval.

The three levels

You earn these one machine at a time. Being certified on the A1 Mini says nothing about the X1C.

Level one Beginner

You can print with an instructor helping you. This is the minimum to touch the machine at all.

Level two Intermediate

You can print with an approved peer helping you — another student who is certified on that machine.

Level three Advanced

You can print on your own. Still with an approved job, but nobody has to be standing there.

The machine order

You work through the printers in sequence, smallest and simplest first. Think of it as unlocking the next level of complexity and size.

Level 1 A1 Mini Where everyone starts.
Level 2 X1C Bigger, faster, enclosed.
Level 3 H2D The largest machine in the room.

You cannot skip a machine. The X1C is not open to you until you have certified on the A1 Mini, and the H2D is not open until the X1C.

How the two combine

Once you complete a level on a machine, you have two ways to go next: up a level on the same machine, or the same level on the next machine. Your choice.

For example: after Beginner on the A1 Mini you may go on to Intermediate on the A1 Mini, or start Beginner on the X1C. Whichever is more useful for the project in front of you.

Most students go up on the A1 Mini first, because printing unsupervised on a machine you know well is worth more day to day than supervised access to a bigger one. But if your project needs the build volume, take the other route.

How you get certified

Training is the free courses at the Bambu Lab Academy, run by the company that makes our printers. Work through the course for the machine you want, then show Mr. Frank what you completed.

Bambu Lab issues a printable Certificate of Completion for each course, so you have something to keep as well as the shop access it unlocks.

  • Do the course for the specific machine — the A1 Mini course does not certify you on the X1C.
  • Bring your completion evidence to Mr. Frank. He records the level.
  • Your current level is tracked by your instructor, not on this site.

Where your status is kept

Ask Mr. Frank — he keeps the class record. It is not published here, and it never will be. Nothing on this site is about any individual student: no names, no progress, no grades. This page is the rule; your record stays where student records belong.

Also worth knowing

Designing for print

Parts that have to move need clearance built in — around 0.3 mm between surfaces that touch. Get it wrong in CAD and the print fuses solid.

Makerspace rules

Eye protection is required in the Makerspace, and open-toed shoes are not allowed in there at all.
