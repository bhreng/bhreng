Worth a look

Links

Reference, channels, model libraries and career data. Not courses — those live on the training page. These are the things worth keeping a tab open for.

Reference you will keep coming back to

Not courses. The things you open for two minutes to check something, then close.

Autodesk Knowledge Network

The official documentation for Fusion, Inventor, AutoCAD, Revit and Forma. When a tool does something you did not expect, this is where the answer actually is.

autodesk.com

Engineering ToolBox

Material properties, thread sizes, conversions, fits and tolerances. Plain, ugly, and correct.

engineeringtoolbox.com

McMaster-Carr

A parts catalogue that doubles as a reference: nearly every listing has a CAD model you can drop straight into an assembly.

mcmaster.com

Arduino language reference

Every function, with an example for each. Faster than searching.

docs.arduino.cc

3D printer safety in schools (UL Chemical Insights)

What a desktop printer actually puts into the air, and what to do about it. Written by Underwriters Laboratories with Georgia Tech for classrooms specifically, not for factories. It is the source behind the filament advice on the SDS page.

greenstrides.org

Worth watching

Channels that show the work rather than only the result.

Practical Engineering

Civil and structural engineering explained with physical models built in a garage. The dam and foundation videos are the best introduction to loads anywhere.

youtube.com

Real Engineering

Longer-form breakdowns of how real aircraft, bridges and machines were actually designed, and what constrained them.

youtube.com

This Old Tony

Machining and fabrication, with the mistakes left in. The dry commentary hides genuinely deep process knowledge.

youtube.com

Stuff Made Here

What happens when mechanical design, electronics and software get pushed well past sensible. A good argument for why the pathways overlap.

youtube.com

Where the free CAD models live

For reference geometry, for parts you do not want to model, and for seeing how other people built something.

Printables

Prusa’s model library. Better moderated than most, and the print settings people post are usually honest.

printables.com

GrabCAD Library

Engineering-grade models rather than printables — motors, bearings, gearboxes, whole machines, in real CAD formats.

grabcad.com

Thingiverse

The oldest and largest. Quality varies wildly; check the "makes" before you trust a model.

thingiverse.com

Where engineering jobs actually are

Worth a look well before senior year, because what these ask for should shape what you learn now.

ONET: Engineering Technologists and Technicians

The US Department of Labor’s occupation database. What the work involves, what it pays, and what it is projected to do.

onetonline.org

Bureau of Labor Statistics: Architecture and Engineering

The official outlook for every engineering occupation, updated yearly.

bls.gov

MassHire Career Centers

Massachusetts career services. The South Shore centre is the closest one to Canton, and they run apprenticeship and job-placement help you can use before you graduate.

mass.gov

Every link here was opened and checked. They still rot — if one is dead, say so and it gets fixed. Suggestions for the shelf are welcome; the bar is that it has to be something you would actually go back to.
